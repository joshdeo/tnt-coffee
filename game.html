The cheat sheet

What makes the perfect shot

These are the numbers most cafes start from. Then they taste and adjust.

18 to 20 g

Dose

Ground coffee in the basket for a double shot. Weigh it to a tenth of a gram.

1:2

Ratio

About 36 g of espresso in the cup from 18 g of coffee. Light roasts can run longer, dark roasts shorter.

25 to 30 s

Time

Counted from the moment the pump starts. Too fast, grind finer. Too slow, grind coarser.

90 to 96 °C

Water

195 to 205 °F, pushed through the coffee at about 9 bar of pressure.

Table salt

Grind

Fine and slightly gritty. Grind size is the main dial for shot time.

About 15 kg

Tamp

Roughly 30 lb, level and the same every time. Even beats forceful.

Sour or bitter

Taste test

Sour and thin means under-extracted. Bitter and drying means over-extracted.

55 to 65 °C

Milk

131 to 149 °F for sweet, silky foam. Past about 70 °C it starts to taste burnt.

Sources: Specialty Coffee Association, Defining the Ever-Changing Espresso, Clive Coffee, Espresso Extraction Time and Ratio, Bean and Brew Tech, What It Takes To Extract The Perfect Cup, Complete Home Barista, Why Does My Milk Taste Burnt.
